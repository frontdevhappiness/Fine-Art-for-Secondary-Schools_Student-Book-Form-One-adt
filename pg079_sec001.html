Watercolour painting

Watercolour painting is the process of applying water-based colours on a surface. There are several types of watercolour paints that an artist can use in painting. These include tube, liquid and dry cake or pan watercolours. Tube watercolours are paints in metal or plastic tubes whereas liquid watercolours consist of pigments suspended in water base solution stored in tins or cans. Dry cake or pan watercolours come in the form of dry cakes in small plastic pans that require more water to make them stable for painting. Other watercolours come in the form of coloured pencils and crayons. Water soluble coloured pencils produce a different effect when applied dry.

Watercolour basic painting techniques

Using watercolours to paint is very challenging because it dries very fast as a result mixing layers becomes difficult. Watercolour layers also spread easily and controlling borders is difficult. One needs a lot of practice to master applying watercolour painting techniques. Painting techniques such as wet-on-dry, wet-on-wet, wash and dry-on-dry need a lot of practice before applying them in actual painting.

Wet-on-dry technique:

This is a technique of applying a wet paintbrush on dry paper. It is suitable for adding details and giving the painting sharp clean edges.

Wet-on-wet technique:

This is a technique almost similar to the wet-on-dry technique except that in wet-on-wet paint is applied on wet paper instead of dry paper. This technique is often applied in painting to blend colours.
