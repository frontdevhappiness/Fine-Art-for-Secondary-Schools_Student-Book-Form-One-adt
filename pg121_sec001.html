After his death, some of his close relatives and friends who became the first and second generation of Tingatinga painters, organised themselves and registered their centre as a cooperative, known as Tingatinga Art Cooperative Society, (TACS).  The centre enabled members to proceed doing what Tingatinga had started.  Currently, in the art field, there are more than 100 Tingatinga artists, who still create more and new Tingatinga painting styles.  (See Figure 5.3 and Figure 5.4).  Tingatinga himself introduced the major style of Single object painting style, his followers evolved more styles such as the Maasai, Historia, Landscape.  Story-telling style, Mashetani, Cartoon, Zimbabwe, Kilimanjaro, Vipepeo, Samaki, Maua, Matunda, Vyumba (Panels), The Aboriginal Australian, Abstract, Mzuguno floral and more other styles.

The founder Edward Said Tingatinga, trained only five first apprentices of the school, most of whom were either his close relatives or friends.  These were Kasper Henrick Tedo, January John Linda, Adeus Manndu Mmatambwe, Abdalah Ajaba, and Simon George Mpata who was Tingatinga’s youngest brother.  After his death, intruders joined the school.  Some of these were the late Omari Amonde, his nephew, Mohamed Abas Charinda, George Lilanga, Hashim Mruta, Damian Msagula, and Said Chilamboni to mention only a few.  This group became the first generation of Tingatinga Painting School.  This generation reproduced the same works of their founder.  Later on, the first generation trained more apprentices, who were, Ally Wasia, Ramadhani Sarange, Said Mitole, and others.  These became the second generation of Tingatinga Painting School.  This emerged group introduced new styles in response to transformations of new subjects related to the new urban and malt-ethnic society of Dar es Salaam.  Examples of such new subjects were crowded and busy streets, the introduction of pictorial depth, and other fine art techniques, a situation which if the founder were alive, no one could have tried.  Currently, Tingatinga is no longer a family or a certain ethnic group’s painting style.  With such freedom of creativity, the school remains with its long-established informal rules of painting Tingatinga art works set by its founder.
