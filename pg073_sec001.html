Colour theory

When objects, things, and other living organisms are exposed to light, we see colours on them. Therefore, colour is what we see on objects or things in light. When you mix, match, compare or contrast colours you apply science. When you creatively apply colours to communicate or express ideas, thoughts and feelings you practically create art.

Properties of colour

Artists paint in different ways and techniques to achieve the desired qualities in their creations. A good understanding of types of colours and their basic properties helps them to achieve a proper colour application, when painting to achieve desired results. This part explains three main properties of colours. These properties are hue, value and intensity.

Hue:

This refers to colour in its original form. It is a unique property or attribute of a certain colour quality. For example, blue, red and yellow are attributes of a colour.

Value:

This is the quality of value lightness or darkness of a colour. When working on hues, value can be altered by adding black or white. The addition of black medium to a hue increases dark or black value, called a shade in colour. Likewise, an addition of white medium to a colour adds a whiter or lighter value called a tint.
