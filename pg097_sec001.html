Techniques of printmaking

Printmaking can be done through various techniques.  Some of them include stencil, relief, intaglio and screen printing.

Stencil method

A stencil uses a thin sheet of hard paper or plastic with a design to be cut on it and printed.  These can be used to produce the cut design on the surface it is placed.  The process is done through applying ink or paint on the stencil, either by using spray paint or opaque paint in which a sponge or brush can be used.  Then, the pigment penetrates through the stencil holes to allow motif patterns to appear on the design.  Figure 4.1 indicates a prepared stencil while Figure 4.2 (a) and Figure 4.2 (b) show the printing process through stencil method.

Materials used for stencil printing

The materials, tools, and equipment used for stencil printing are:

Stencil film: This is a hard paper or plastic used to create a stencil.  The cut design is set on it.

Carving knife: This is a flat sharp-edged metal used to cut out the design from a stencil film.

Masking tape: This is made of thin paper used to stick together stencils and paper to be printed.

Printing ink: This is a fluid or paste used for printing on a surface.  When they are used, they leave a particular mark on a design.

Squeegee: It is a tool used for evenly applying ink to the stencil.

Printing papers: These are specialised printmaking papers which are used for relief printing.  They are chosen for their ability to hold ink well and produce high-quality prints.  The thickness and texture of the paper can influence the final appearance of the print.

Printing table: A flat and stable surface for inking and stencil printing.

Steps in making stencil prints

Brainstorm the design: Plan the design to be printed by sketching the main concept of the design.

Design creation: Finish the design using a sketch as a guide by making some improvements; this may be done on a computer using design software.
