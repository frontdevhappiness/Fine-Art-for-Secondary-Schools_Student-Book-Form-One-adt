Relief printing

Relief printing is the process of producing a relief print. A relief print is an image produced by a raised surface from the block plate. The completed carved design is inked by using a roller then transferred to an intended place by applying pressure to the entire plate evenly. In most cases, relief printing is done by using woodcut and linocut. However, artists can improvise other accessible and available materials.

Relief printing involves several processes including creating an image for printing, transferring the image and cutting the plate to reveal an intended image to be printed. An intended image is first drawn on a flat plate. The printing plate in relief printing is created by removing, or deleting areas that are not intended to be printed or in other words remove the background so that it remains white during the printing process. The remaining areas of the original surface receive the ink. It is a printing process whereby areas that you don’t want to print are physically removed. The remaining raised areas are inked and printed. Relief printing can be done by hand or a printing press.

Materials used in relief printing

There are various materials used in relief printing as discussed here:

Printing plate:

This is sometimes known as printing block. It is the most critical material in relief printing. It can be made from various materials, including:

Wood: Woodblocks are commonly used, and different types of wood, like linoleum, plywood, or hardwood, can be chosen based on the desired results.
Linoleum: Also known as lino-cut, linoleum blocks are softer and easier to carve than woodblocks, making them popular for beginners and artists looking for a quicker process.
Rubber: Rubber blocks or soft sculpture blocks offer a flexible and easy-to-carve surface.

Other materials an artist can improvise, include potatoes, cassava or linoleum.

Carving tools:

Relief printmakers use various tools to carve the block and create the image. Some of these include carving knives, chisels, trimming knives, sandpapers, razorblades and any other related carving tools.
