Etching needle: An etching needle is a pointed tool used for creating fine lines and details on the metal plate during the engraving process.

Burin: A burin is a specialised engraving tool with a sharp, V-shaped tip.  It is used to incise broader lines and remove metal from the plate to create shaded areas.

Roulette: This is a small, spiked wheel-like tool used to create a random texture or pattern on the plate.  This technique is often used in backgrounds or areas where shading is required.

Scraper/burnisher: The scraper is used to remove excess ink from the plate during the wiping process, while the burnisher is used to smoothen and polish the surface of the plate before printing.

Intaglio printing press: This is a heavy-duty printing press specifically designed for intaglio printing.  The press exerts significant pressure to transfer the ink from the engraved plate to the paper.

Wiping cloth/pads: These kinds of cloths or pads are used for wiping excess ink from the plate, leaving ink only in the engraved lines and grooves.

Dampening system: A system to moisten the printing paper, ensuring it remains damp during the printing process.  This enhances the paper’s ability to pick up the ink from the plate.

Drying racks: Used to lay out and dry the freshly printed sheets of paper.

The intaglio printing steps

The intaglio printing process involves the following steps:

Plate preparation: A metal plate, typically made of copper, zinc, or steel, is meticulously prepared for engraving.  Its surface is polished and cleaned to ensure a smooth and even base.

Engraving: Skilled artisans, known as engravers or etchers, use specialised tools to cut or incise the image into the plate’s surface.  This process involves creating fine lines and varying depths to represent different tones and shades in the final print.  The engraved plate becomes the master image that will be transferred onto the paper.
