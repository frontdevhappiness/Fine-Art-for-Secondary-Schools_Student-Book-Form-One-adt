Makonde sculpture movement

Makonde sculpture originated from the Makonde people living on the plateau, south of the Ruvuma river in Mozambique.  These people migrated northward into Tanzania and joined the curio trade that began to emerge in 1950s and 1960s in Mtwara and Dar es Salaam.  Traditionally, the Makonde people create household together with ritual objects such as masks used in initiation ceremonies.  The contemporary Makonde artists create sculptures for business purposes rather than for society’s use.  Makonde sculptures are carved from the ebony hardwood which is known as mpingo in Swahili language.  The mpingo wood is characterised by black colour.  However, other wood such as mpolopolo, mwarobaini and mninga just to mention a few are used to create sculptures.  Makonde sculptures are not only popular in Tanzania but in the whole world due to their unique carving styles.  The sculptures are the attractions for tourists visiting Tanzania.  Indeed, there are plenty of Makonde sculptures in museums and various exhibitions worldwide.  For instance, China holds the largest collection of Makonde artworks in the world.  Due to such popularity, today, Makonde sculpture in Tanzania has attracted artists including the non-Makonde people who are making their living through creating Makonde art.
