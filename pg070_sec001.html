For example, turpentine, linseed oil and kerosene are solvents for oil paints, while water is a solvent for watercolours. Solvents are also used to clean brushes. Paint is the main medium for painting which is available in various packages such as in tubes, tins, powder or plastic containers. Paints are categorised based on the kind of solvent they use. These include; oil paints, watercolours, acrylics, gouache, and tempera.

Oil paint: This is an oil-based paint. It was first used in the 1400s and has continued to be one of the most popular media of painting applied globally.

Watercolour: This is a painting medium in which the pigment is blended with water. Watercolour is the most sensitive of all the painting media because it dries quickly. It also reacts to the slightest touch of the artist and can become an overworked mess in a moment if not used carefully.

Acrylic: This is a quick-drying water-based synthetic paint, it is a manufactured paint with plastic binders. Acrylic is very versatile and can be applied in both thick and thin glaze. Once it is dried it becomes resistant to water.

Gouache is an opaque watercolour medium made of natural pigment with gum Arabic or dextrin binder. It can be diversely used in making a range of artworks including murals illustrations and comics.

Tempera: This is a painting medium in which the pigment is mixed with egg yolk and water. Tempera is a very old medium which has been used for thousands of years. For instance, in the early Christian art era, tempera was used extensively to paint images of religious icons.

Equipment for painting

Equipment for painting means tools or instruments that are used in the painting activities. These include:

Painting brushes: These are brushes used to apply paint or colour on the surface of a paper, board or canvas. There are different sizes and shapes of brushes. The shape and size of a brush is important because each brush is made for particular type and size of colour stroke. The size of a brush is determined by the type, shape and size of its bristle. There are special brushes for different paints or media. Sables are the most common type of brushes used by artists. This is because they are made to work with varieties of colours such as tempera, oil colours, acrylics and watercolours, without getting easily broken or diminished.
