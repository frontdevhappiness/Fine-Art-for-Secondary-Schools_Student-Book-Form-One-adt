Handling drawing materials and equipment

It is very important to take good care of all drawing materials and equipment before, during and after class or studio activities. This is because the value of a work of art relies on the quality of materials and the equipment used to create it. Besides, art materials and equipment are fragile and very expensive. It is advised to keep art materials and tools in a storage or studio where safety and quality of the equipment are properly observed. The following is safety guidance on handling art materials and equipment:

  • (a)

    Drawing materials and equipment should be stored in a safe place when they are not in use.

  • (b)

    Drawing books should be kept away from any liquid materials to avoid wetting.

  • (c)

    Flammable tools like rubber erasers, plastics, rulers, and oil colours that use turpentine should be kept away from any source of heat or fire.

  • (d)

    Brushes should be cleaned well with proper solvents after each use.

  • (e)

    Liquid materials such as fixative sprays, linseed oils, and solvents should not be left open to avoid contamination by dusting to prevent them from drying out due to air exposure.

  • (f)

    Drawing papers should not be folded before and after use.

  • (g)

    Do not use sharp tools such as a razor blade, or a knife for cutting anything on the drawing-board surface or table. This includes cutting stencils and trimming papers. More often, such acts distort the surface of a drawing board.

  • (h)

    Clean erasers with clean-soft-cloth material after use and pack them into their package or container. Doing so helps to keep erasers clean for the next user.

  • (i)

    Do not use pins for fitting your paper on the drawing board, instead use artistic clips or artistic tape.

  • (j)

    Drawing materials containing fluid, for example, fountain marker pens should be kept facing down or laid horizontally to avoid leakage.
