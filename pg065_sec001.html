Traditional technique of creating a simple drawing

This is a method of using ordinary charcoal to create drawings. Ordinary charcoal can easily be obtained from the artist’s environment without or with little expenses.

Ordinary charcoal

Charcoal is a lightweight black carbon residue created by intensely burning wood in low-oxygen environments to eliminate all water and other unrelated elements. It is mostly used as a source of heat used for cooking activities. It has a dark appearance and it can easily be obtained from a charcoal vendor or leftover firewood (See Figure 2.37). Similar to the modern charcoal we get from art supply stores; ordinary charcoal comes in different levels of hardness from powder to hard. Although it is challenging to determine these levels; it is achievable through a pressing test on paper. The charcoal’s colour can indicate whether it is soft or hard. The darker marks represent soft charcoal, whilst the lighter marks represent hard charcoal. The crushed charcoal is used to create the powder.
