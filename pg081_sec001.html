Approaches to painting

Artists obtain their paintings subject matter either from memory, imagination or observation. When an artist decides on the source of his content, he or she makes sketches about the ideas, thoughts, feelings or imagination and prepares all necessary tools and equipment for his work. Similarly, the artist makes or preparations, when painting from direct observation of nature or experience.

Painting from memory: This is an approach to painting in which a work of art is made to depict bits and pieces of images of a subject recalled or memorised from past experiences. When painting from memory, it becomes very difficult to compare the source of the work of art to its results.

Painting from imagination: This is a little different to painting from memory except all that an artist is creating in the final artwork is not a representation of anything existed before. Imaginative painting is made of images, shapes, and colour schemes from scratch, based on visualised ideas in the mind of the artist.

Painting from observation: This is an approach that artists use when they want to paint a subject or an object correctly but not precisely. This approach is applied to other approaches such as still-life painting. It involves observing various subjects, such as landscapes, seascapes, animals, plants and even still life set up. The difference between painting from observation and still life painting is on representational attributes of a still life approach which are not necessary in the painting from observation.

Still life painting: It is a style of painting involving arranged inanimate objects which are displayed over a flat surface or a table. The artist is supposed to produce an exact copy of objects displayed in the form of a painted picture. Figure 3.11 shows a still-life painting.
