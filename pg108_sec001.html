Materials used in screen printing process

The following are materials used in the screen-printing process:

Screen mesh: The mesh is available in different thread counts or “mesh counts” per inch, which affects the level of detail that can be achieved in the print.  The mesh count is chosen based on the artwork’s complexity and the type of ink being used.

Emulsion: Emulsion is a light-sensitive liquid applied to the screen mesh.  It becomes solid when exposed to UV light, creating the stencil.

Transparent film or vellum: The design is printed on transparent film or vellum, which is used to transfer the artwork onto the emulsion-coated screen during exposure.

Screen printing ink: Screen printing inks come in various types, including water-based, plastisol, and discharge inks.  The choice of ink depends on the printing surface (e.g., fabric, paper) and the desired effect (e.g., opaque, soft, or vibrant colours).

Substrate: The material being printed on, such as fabric, paper, glass, metal, or plastic, is known as the substrate.

Clean-up supplies: Various chemicals and solvents are used for cleaning the screen, removing excess ink, and reclaiming screens for reuse.

Registration guides: These guides help align different colour layers precisely during multicolour printing, ensuring that the final design looks sharp and well-defined.

Note: A motif used in the screen method, should be printed with heavy and quality ink.  Kerosene or any cooking oil must be available, as it helps to moisten a screen motif on paper making it easy to transfer the motif from a paper to the screen.  The mixing ratio of a photo emulsion is to some extent estimation, but the simple estimation is as follows; one teaspoonful of sensitiser should be mixed and dissolved in 500mls of water by shaking well to let the sensitiser dissolve in water.  Then take five teaspoons of photo emulsion plus two teaspoons of sensitizer.
